METHODOLOGY NOTE — NPP ESTIMATES
NPP crossover figures are modeled approximations, not directly measured data. The CA Secretary of State does not publish vote totals by voter registration type. Estimates are derived using the following method:

NPP ballots returned: 1,131,256 (16% of 6,967,782 NP+Other ballots mailed) per post-election politicaldata.com tracker update. Election-night figure was 990,429 (14%), reflecting VBM returns only prior to in-person election day voting.

(1) Republican base votes estimated as: R ballots returned (1,736,030 per politicaldata.com VBM tracker, updated post-election) × Hilton's share of total R field vote (68.4%) = ~1,071,800 R-registered votes for Hilton.

(2) NPP/crossover residual = Hilton total votes (1,386,966) minus estimated R base (~1,071,800) = ~315,000 non-Republican votes, minus estimated ~45–55k Democratic crossovers = ~260–270k estimated NPP votes.

(3) R aggregate overperformance: Republicans were 33.4% of the electorate (1,736,030 of 5,195,070 total ballots cast) but received 40.6% of all governor race votes (denominator: ~4,994,976) — an +8.9pt gap representing ~450–480k votes attributable to NPP crossover.

ELECTION NIGHT FINAL VERDICT

Hilton finishes first at 27.8%, Becerra second at 25.4%. Both advance to the November general under California's top-two system. Steyer is eliminated at 19.6%, trailing Becerra by 288,063 votes — a gap that never meaningfully threatened to close.

Democrats won the aggregate party vote by 865,189 votes — a 17.3-point advantage — yet Hilton finishes first. Republican consolidation (96.3% of R votes behind top-2 candidates) vs Democratic fragmentation (24 candidates splitting 58% of the vote, top-2 Dems capturing only 77.6%) is the defining structural story of the night.

Hilton overperformed his PPIC polling baseline by +7.7 points. With Republicans making up only 30.2% of the electorate, his 27.8% required substantial NPP crossover. Republicans collectively won ~47–49% of NPP voters who turned out — a significant achievement given 25.14% registration share — but NPP turnout was 16% (updated post-election per politicaldata.com), meaning the NPP voters who showed up skewed older and more conservative than the full NPP universe.

Latino turnout at 13% against 6.65M ballots mailed is the most consequential number for the canvass period. As late VBM and provisional ballots are processed through July 10, Becerra's certified total will rise meaningfully. The 17 counties still in U-status represent a structurally Democratic outstanding universe.

November outlook: Becerra is a clear structural favorite. The Democratic registration advantage (44.96% vs 25.14%), high-turnout general election dynamics, normalized Latino participation, and full Democratic consolidation in a two-person race all favor him. Hilton's path requires sustaining 30%+ of NPP voters in a very different electorate from tonight's low-turnout primary sample. Tonight was Hilton's best-case scenario — he still finished second in total party votes by nearly 900,000.
