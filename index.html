Canvass analysis — Becerra takes the lead

In roughly 72 hours of canvassing, the race has completely flipped. Hilton's election night peak lead of +120,084 has turned into a Becerra lead of +22,048 — a swing of 142,132 votes. The canvass has added 1,322,125 governor race votes since election night, running at roughly 61% Democratic.

Every canvass batch has been majority Democratic. Hilton's individual batch share has ranged from 17.9% to 25.1% — consistently below his 27.8% election night share. The C6 batch (Jun 5, 4:24p) was decisive: 396k votes at 60.8% D wiped out the remaining Hilton advantage in a single drop.

Becerra's vote share has risen from 25.4% election night to 26.7% at C7. Hilton has declined from 27.8% to 26.4%. The lines have crossed — Becerra now leads both in raw votes and vote share percentage.

Outlook: with 3,055,949 ballots still outstanding statewide — heavily concentrated in D-leaning LA, Sacramento, Santa Clara, and San Francisco — Becerra's certified lead is expected to grow through the July 10 certification deadline. The structural math strongly favors a widening Becerra advantage.

Hilton November framing: Led wire-to-wire on election night. First Republican to lead a California governor primary since 2006. NPP crossover appeal proven — won ~47–49% of independent primary voters. Trails by only 22k with 3M+ ballots still to count. Path to November: hold all Bianco R votes, sustain 30%+ of NPP in 16M-voter general electorate.
Becerra November framing: Closed 142k votes in 72 hours of canvassing to take the lead. Canvass trajectory is a powerful momentum story. Full D consolidation in two-person race + normalized Latino turnout (23% → typical 30%+) + 19-point registration advantage = heavy favorite in November.
